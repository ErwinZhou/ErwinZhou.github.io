## Overview

Implemented and tuned an Adaptive Bitrate (ABR) algorithm for video streaming, targeting realistic
last-mile network conditions rather than a clean, high-bandwidth link. The work sat on top of a
DASH-based video streaming testbed using the dash.js player, containerized with Docker.

## Problem

ABR algorithms have to make a real-time trade-off every few seconds: start playback quickly, keep
video quality high, and avoid rebuffering — but you usually can't maximize all three at once,
especially over a variable, imperfect last-mile connection. The right choice depends on how you
weigh startup latency, quality, and stability against each other.

## What I built

- A **buffer-based ABR algorithm** (in the style of BBA-0), selecting bitrate primarily from
  player buffer occupancy rather than throughput estimation alone.
- Integration with a **DASH-based video streaming testbed** running the **dash.js** player.
- A **Docker**-based setup for reproducing realistic last-mile network conditions during testing.
- Tuning aimed at maximizing overall **Quality of Experience (QoE)** — balancing startup latency,
  average video quality, and playback stability (rebuffering, quality switches).

## Results

- An ABR algorithm that measurably trades off startup latency, quality, and stability, evaluated
  under realistic last-mile network conditions on the testbed.

## Trade-offs & limitations

- A purely buffer-based approach reacts to what's already happened in the buffer rather than
  predicting future throughput — a hybrid buffer + throughput-based approach could do better under
