## Overview

A reliable transport protocol built from scratch in C, modeled closely on TCP Reno, running on
top of UDP and evaluated under real, lossy network conditions rather than an idealized simulator.

## Problem

UDP gives you an unreliable, unordered datagram service — no delivery guarantees, no congestion
awareness. To get TCP-like reliability over it, you need to build your own sliding window,
retransmission, congestion control, and flow control on top, and have it behave sanely when the
network is slow, lossy, or bursty.

## What I built

- A **sliding-window** reliable delivery layer on top of raw UDP sockets.
- **Congestion control modeled on TCP Reno**: slow start, congestion avoidance, and fast
  retransmit/fast recovery on duplicate ACKs.
- **Flow control** so a fast sender can't overrun a slow receiver's buffer.
- **RTT estimation** feeding retransmission timeout calculation, so timeouts adapt to real network
  latency rather than a fixed value.
- **Packet loss recovery** via retransmission, tested against real network conditions rather than
  a clean simulated link.

## Results

- A transport protocol that recovers cleanly from loss, reordering, and congestion while running
  over real network paths, behaving qualitatively like TCP Reno.

## Trade-offs & limitations

- Reno-style congestion control is simpler than modern algorithms (e.g. CUBIC or BBR) and leaves
  throughput on the table under some loss patterns — a natural next step to explore.
